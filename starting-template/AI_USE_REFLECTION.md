# End-of-Module AI Use Reflection

Mandatory | Submitted once, at the end of the module | Not marked | Individual work | Module code PRG1

## Purpose

This replaces a per-task AI-use log with a single short reflection, written once at the end of the module, covering your use of AI tools across the whole assignment. It exists to capture something a log of individual prompts cannot: an honest, retrospective account of how you actually used AI, how you checked what it gave you, and what you learned from doing that.

You do not need to log, note down, or record anything about AI use as you go through the assignment, task by task. There is nothing to keep track of during the module. Complete this reflection once, only at the end, looking back over the assignment as a whole.

Using an AI tool does not affect your mark either way, on any task, and this reflection is not marked and is not consulted when marking any individual task. It stands alone as evidence of your AI literacy and critical use, not as a record to be audited line by line. If you did not use an AI tool at all, say so; that is a complete, valid answer to every question below.

Completing and submitting this reflection is a mandatory part of your assignment submission, even though its content is not marked and does not affect your grade. A submission without it will be treated as incomplete.

## Write your reflection

Answer each of the four questions below in a short paragraph, two to five sentences is normally enough. Use the guiding questions under each one to help you structure your answer; you do not need to address every guiding question individually, and you should write in your own words, specific to what you actually did across this assignment, not a general statement about AI tools.

### 1. How did you use AI tools across the assignment, if at all?

- Which task or tasks did you use it on, and which did you not?
- What did you actually ask it to do: explain something, write or suggest code, help debug, check an answer, something else?
- Roughly how much did you rely on it: a starting point you rewrote yourself, a check on work you had already done, or something closer to a first draft?

>

### 2. How did you check whether what it gave you was actually correct?

- Did you run it, test it, or trace through it by hand before relying on it?
- Did you compare it against the task specification, the test file, or your own understanding?
- Did you find anything wrong with what it gave you, and if so, how did you find it?

>

### 3. What did you learn from using it, or from checking its output?

- Did checking its output teach you something about the code, the concept, or your own understanding?
- Did you notice anything about where AI tools tend to be more or less reliable?
- Would you trust it the same way again on a similar task, or would you check differently next time?

>

### 4. Where was it useful, and where was it not, or where did you choose not to use it?

- Was there a task where it did not help, or made things harder rather than easier?
- Was there a task where you deliberately chose not to use it? Why?
- Looking back over the whole assignment, is there anything you would do differently next time?

>
